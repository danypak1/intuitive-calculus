Four ideas, one thread

Everything in this course is really
just four ideas, dressed up differently.

You don't need forty-one units of intuition — you need four. Every technique you'll learn is a variation on one of these. Play with each one until it clicks; the formulas in the units will just be this, written more precisely.

1. Limits 2. The Derivative 3. The Integral 4. The Fundamental Theorem
1 Limits

What a function is doing near a point — even if it's undefined right there

The function below has a hole at x=2 — it's genuinely undefined there. But drag the point toward x=2 from either side, and watch what value it keeps approaching anyway. That approached value is the limit — it doesn't care that the function itself has a gap.

x =0.00
f(x) =2.00
f(2) itselfundefined
limit as x→24
The idea: f(x) = (x²−4)/(x−2) simplifies to x+2 everywhere except exactly at x=2, where it's a 0/0 puzzle. The graph has a hole, not a jump — so from both sides, the value keeps closing in on 4. That's the whole definition of a limit: not "what happens at the point," but "what happens as you get arbitrarily close to it."
2 The Derivative

A tangent line is a secant line that ran out of distance

Fix one point on the curve. Pick a second point some distance h away, and draw the line through both — that's a secant line, and its slope is an average rate of change. Now shrink h toward zero. Watch the slope settle on a single number — that's the derivative, the instantaneous rate of change at that one point.

point P(1, 1)
h =1.60
secant slope =3.60
f′(1) (true slope)2
The idea: for f(x)=x², the secant slope works out to exactly 2+h — plug in any h and check. As h→0, that expression heads straight to 2, no matter which side you approach from. That's f′(1)=2: not a rate over some interval, but a rate at a single instant.
3 The Integral

Area under a curve is just a lot of thin rectangles, added up

You can't find the exact area under a curve with a rectangle — curves aren't straight. But you can chop the region into many thin rectangles, add up their areas, and get a good estimate. Slide n up, and watch the jagged staircase hug the curve tighter and tighter.

estimated area =—
exact area (calculus) =36.000
gap—
The idea: this is a Riemann sum. As n→∞, the rectangles get infinitely thin and the estimate becomes exact — that limiting process is the definite integral, the area under −0.5x²+4x from 0 to 6, which works out to exactly 36. The Fundamental Theorem (next module) is what lets you skip this whole rectangle-counting process entirely.
4 The Fundamental Theorem

The slope of the area function is the original function

Let A(x) be the running total area under f from 0 up to x — it grows as x moves right. Drag x and watch two things happen together: the shaded area under f grows, and the point tracing A(x) moves. The slope of that traced curve, at any instant, is exactly the height of f at that same instant.

x =3.00
f(x) (height of curve)2.50
A(x) (area so far)5.25
slope of A at x2.50
The idea: f(x) and the slope of A at x always match, at every value of x you drag to. That's the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus: A′(x)=f(x). It's the bridge that connects Module 2 (derivative) and Module 3 (area) into a single idea — differentiating undoes integrating.
